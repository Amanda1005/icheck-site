iCheck Privacy Policy

Effective date: 9 October 2026
Developer: NianYa Weng
Contact: icheck.help@gmail.com

iCheck helps you check whether a message, link or phone call might be a scam. This policy explains what happens to your information when you use the app. We wrote it in plain English. If anything is unclear, please contact us.

The short version

  • No account, no name, no phone number. You don't sign up for iCheck.
  • We don't keep the messages you check. A message is analysed and then discarded. We keep only a record of the result (for example, "high risk, asked for a code"), not the words.
  • Screenshots stay on your phone. The text is read from a screenshot on your phone, and only that text is sent.
  • No advertising and no tracking. iCheck has no ads, no tracking tools and no analytics companies. We never sell your information.
  • Some information leaves Australia. To analyse a message, we send its text to an AI service in the United States. To check links, we send only the link to two security services. Details are below.

1. What we collect and why

When you check a message, link or screenshot

  • The text you check. We need it to look for warning signs. It is analysed and then discarded, and our servers don't store it.
  • Screenshots. The text is read on your phone (Apple's on-device text recognition). The image itself never leaves your phone.
  • A record of the result. This is kept to count your daily checks and to measure and improve how well iCheck works. It holds:
  • the risk level and score, and the warning-sign codes (for example, "asks for a code")
  • the language and type of input (text, link or screenshot)
  • which version of our rules and AI was used, and how long the check took
  • a one-way fingerprint of the text (a "hash"). We can't turn it back into your message. It lets us recognise that the same text was checked again, for example when you answer a follow-up question.
  • Your answers to follow-up questions, such as "Did you ask for this code yourself?" These can change the result. The answer is kept as part of the result record.
  • Your app language (English, 繁體中文, 简体中文 or Español), so the explanation comes back in that language.

When you check a phone number

The number you type stays on your phone. The app downloads a list of phone numbers that banks, government agencies and other organisations publish themselves, and compares your number with that list on your phone. We never receive the number you check.

When you look at Latest scams

The app asks our server for the latest scam alerts. Our server gets them from the public Scamwatch news feed; nothing about you is sent to Scamwatch. If you tap "Read on Scamwatch", the Scamwatch website opens in your browser, and its own privacy policy applies there.

To make the app work fairly

  • A random device number. The app creates a random number when you first use it. It is not your phone's serial number, your Apple ID or your phone number. We use it to limit everyone to 10 checks a day. On our servers we keep only a scrambled (hashed) version of it. On your phone it is kept in the secure keychain and stays there even if you delete and reinstall the app, so the daily limit isn't reset that way.
  • Your internet (IP) address. We use it only to stop too many checks coming from one place at once. We keep only a scrambled (hashed) version, for about two minutes.
  • Proof that it's the real app (Apple App Attest). To stop people using automated scripts against our service, the app asks Apple's App Attest service to confirm it is the genuine iCheck app on a real iPhone. Your phone creates a security key that never leaves it; Apple confirms the key, and we keep only its public part and a counter, to check that later requests come from the same app. This tells us nothing about who you are. If it isn't available, the app still works, but with a simpler check (without the AI) and fewer checks a day.

If you answer the feedback questions

If you tell us whether a result was helpful, or what you did with the message, we keep your answer with the result record. This helps us find mistakes. Answering is optional.

2. Who we share information with

We don't sell or rent your information, and we don't share it for advertising. We use these services to run iCheck:

Service What they receive Why Where
Anthropic (Claude AI) The text you check To help analyse the message and explain the result United States
Google Safe Browsing Only the web links found in the message (not the message) To check the links against Google's list of dangerous websites United States
URLhaus by abuse.ch Only the web links found in the message (not the message) To check the links against a list of websites that spread harmful software Switzerland
Short-link services (such as bit.ly) Only a short link found in the message, asked by our server, not your phone To see where the short link really leads Depends on the service
Apple (App Attest) From your phone: a request to confirm the app is genuine. Nothing about you or your messages To stop automated misuse of our service Apple's servers
Scamwatch (Australian Government) Nothing about you: our server reads its public news feed To show Latest scams Australia
Fly.io Everything our servers process, including your internet address when you connect To run our servers Sydney, Australia
Neon The result records described in section 1 To store our database Sydney, Australia

About Anthropic: Anthropic states that, for its API service, it deletes inputs and outputs within 30 days and does not use them to train its AI models by default. See Anthropic's Privacy Center (privacy.claude.com).

We may also share information if Australian law requires us to.

3. Information sent overseas

The text you check is sent to Anthropic in the United States, and links may be sent to Google (United States) and abuse.ch (Switzerland). The text is processed only to give you a result. Before you check a message, you can remove details you'd rather not send, such as your name or account number. The check still works without them.

4. How long we keep information

Information How long
The text you check Not stored by us. Anthropic deletes it within 30 days.
Result records, follow-up and feedback answers 12 months, then deleted
Daily check counts 7 days
Scrambled IP address About 2 minutes
Link check results Up to 6 hours, in our server's memory only

Phone numbers you check Never sent to us

5. On your phone

  • Past checks. The app keeps your last 20 checks on your phone so you can look at them again. They are not sent to us. You can delete them at any time in Settings → Past checks → Delete all past checks. Deleting the app also deletes them. Like other app data, they are included in your phone's backups.
  • Your settings (language, text size, and light or dark appearance) and the random device number are stored on your phone.
  • Sharing iCheck. If you tap "Share iCheck", your phone's own share menu opens. We don't see who you share it with.

6. Security

Information sent between the app and our servers is encrypted. Our servers and database are in Sydney. Access is limited to the developer. We store scrambled (hashed) versions of the device number and IP address instead of the originals.

7. Your choices and rights

  • You can use iCheck without giving us your name, email or phone number.
  • You can delete your past checks on your phone at any time.
  • Because we don't know who you are, we usually can't find records about you. If you want to ask about your information or make a complaint, contact us at icheck.help@gmail.com. We'll reply within 30 days.
  • If you're not satisfied with our reply, you can contact the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) at www.oaic.gov.au or on 1300 363 992.

8. Children

iCheck is not designed for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect information from them.

9. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll update the date at the top. If the change is important, we'll also let you know in the app.
